## Expected content

Your final report should include the following content:

- **Introduction:** Describe and motivate the problem that you are studying. Summarise the approach taken in the project. Use references to existing literature to explain why the approach is suitable for the chosen problem.
- **Literature review / Background:** Conduct a literature review to understand the current state-of-the-art in the complex system that you are studying. Your literature review will inform the theoretical approach that you are taking to solve the problem, as well as the critical analysis of your results. You may want to include a separate Literature Review or Background section in the report, but citations in your Introduction and throughout your report may also be sufficient. (You can discuss with your supervisor which approach is best for your work).
- **Methodology and implementation:** Describe research/design methods and materials used to solve the Complex Systems problems that you are studying. This section should be sufficiently detailed, so that the results of your project could be reproduced.
- **Results:** Present the results in the appropriate formats, e.g., tables and figures.
- **Analysis, discussion and conclusions:** Analyse the results using appropriate techniques. Interpret and critically discuss your results, making connections with existing theory and/or previous work. Explain how your contribution solves the problem in a novel way. Depending on the project, analysis/discussion can be integrated with the Results rather than a separate section (you can discuss with your supervisor which approach is best for your work). Discuss the limitations of the work and suggest directions for future work.
- **Appendix:** Please submit any other technical material that does not fit naturally in the main text in an Appendix. These sections do not contribute to the word count, and are generally not incorporated in the marking. You can include programming code where relevant, although the main code that you produce for the project should normally be shared separately with your supervisor.

These do not necessarily need to be separate sections in your report; for example Results and Discussion components can often be integrated.

The level you pitch your report at should be as though to a peer in the Master of Complex Systems program. Your report will be marked from this perspective. Whilst your supervisor will likely have a deep understanding of the concepts involved in your project, your report may be read by other markers or other students in future, and the assessment is checking whether you are able to explain and discuss the concepts and results in a way that is accessible for them.

The standard marking guide for the report is consistent across all capstone/research unit codes; you can see the marking guide by navigating to the submission point for your project(s) via your Assignments page.

Your report will be marked by your supervisor, and since every project is different there may be good reasons that the standard marking guide is not used by your supervisor. You should clarify with your supervisor whether there are reasons to utilise a different format and/or marking guide for your project.

Since your presentation will be delivered before your final report, make sure to utilise the feedback you have received from your presentation here — your supervisor will be expecting you to have done so!

## Guidelines on AI usage

**Acknowledgment of AI Use:** You must properly acknowledge any AI assistance in your submissions. This includes citing the specific AI tools used and describing how they contributed to the work. For the report this can be an Appendix in the submitted PDF.

**Record-Keeping Requirement:** Students who use AI tools for unsecured assessments should maintain a record of their interactions with these tools (e.g., prompts, responses, or generated outputs). These records may be requested by faculty to verify the student's contribution and the extent of AI involvement. (Many such AI tools have a function to 'archive' the record so that they won't get deleted automatically).

### Frequently asked questions

- **I am undertaking 2 units of study (12 credit points) of capstone/research in a single semester, with each unit directed towards a separate project — how do I submit two reports, and how are the marks given for the correct project?** If you go to your Assignments page, you will see a separate submission point for each capstone/research unit of study that you are enrolled in, and you will need to submit separately for each of them. Make sure you submit the report from each project to the matching unit of study.
- **I am undertaking 2 units of study (12 credit points) of capstone/research in a single semester and directing both towards a single project in a deeper fashion — how does this change my report assessment?** See the answer in the section "Considerations in selecting a project" on the Supervisor and Project Selection page.
- **I am working in a pair on my project — how does this change my report assessment?** See the answer in the section "Considerations in selecting a project" on the Supervisor and Project Selection page.
- **Can I go over the word limit?** We do not encourage this, to limit the amount of work you are doing, to cap the work required to mark the report, and to keep the reports comparable. In some circumstances your supervisor (as the marker) may deem this appropriate, e.g. if your report is intended to be used as the basis for a scientific article. In those cases, the supervisor may give permission to go over the word limit, but must take any advantage given by the extra word limit into account when marking.
